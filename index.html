What changed — week to 3 September
  • Cleared, then contested, then overtaken. Trump said on 25 August that all mines had been removed; a JMIC advisory the same day held Hormuz at SEVERE and Gharibabadi called the claim “propaganda intended to create calm in global energy markets.” CENTCOM's Adm. Brad Cooper made the clearance formal on 28 August. The corridor was never announced by Oman and never opened.
  • Direct fire resumed. US forces struck two IRGC rocket launchers on Larak Island (30 Aug) as the Guards prepared to fire rocket-delivered sea mines; Iran answered against Jordan. On 1 September CENTCOM hit an estimated 100 IRGC targets and Iran fired 13 ballistic missiles into Jordanian airspace, 10 intercepted, plus drones on Gulf bases. It is the heaviest exchange in over a month.
  • Both curves broke, together, in two sessions. Brent's basis went −$0.90 (26 Aug) to −$3.54 (2 Sep) and Dubai's to −$10.08, with Oman at −$8.66 — the widest prompt premiums either series has printed. The outright rose with them, $86.94 to $92.09, +5.9%. This continues August's backwardation rather than reversing it.
  • The reserve drew again; the taper is four weeks old. −3.1 mb to 286.6 mb (28 Aug), 40.1% of authorised capacity, at 0.45 mb/day. Contracted barrels ~4.7 mb; cushion 86.6 mb, 19–28 weeks.

01Three claims, tracked weekly

Each claim carries its own evidence, its own status, and what it would imply if it holds. Status is set against the prior week, not against the paper's opening position.

The thesis, in one paragraph

In March 2026 the United States authorised a 172 mb SPR release, structured as an exchange rather than a sale, inside a coordinated 400 mb IEA action against the Hormuz closure. This paper has argued since v1 that it was never sized to replace lost supply — it cannot clear a 9–13 mb/day shortfall at any deliverable rate — and that its observable function is active price suppression: pressing the prompt market with finite barrels and the forward curve with announcement effects, to buy time for diplomacy. The barrels and the signal are spent in the same act. That act is complete: ~4.7 mb of contracted barrels remain, and what now moves the price is ordnance.

The release was sized to suppress price, not to replace supply
holding

Realised lift averaged 0.79 mb/day across the programme against an advertised 1.43, peaking at ~1.31 in the three record weeks of May. DOE's own movement ledger confirms 116.6 mb released March–August against no return barrels since February. The draw slowed again to −3.1 mb, 286.6 mb in the week to 28 August — 0.45 mb/day, a fourth consecutive taper, consistent with a programme running out of contracted volume rather than one throttled back by choice. Roughly 4.7 mb remain of the ~133.5 mb awarded. Commercial crude drew faster than the strategic reserve over the same week.

→ Implication · the taper is exhaustion, not restraint. At this pace the contracted book empties within a fortnight, and the reserve stops being an instrument at all.

Suppression is visible in the prompt–physical spread, and it is now failing
fired

Both curves broke together. Brent's basis went −$0.90 (26 Aug) to −$3.54 (2 Sep), Dubai's to −$10.08 and Oman's to −$8.66 — the widest prompt premiums any of the three has printed. The outright rose with them, $86.94 to $92.09, so level and structure are moving together. This extends the backwardation the paper first identified in August rather than reversing it, and it arrived with the resumption of direct fire, not with buffer exhaustion.

→ Implication · the divergence of the prior week has closed the hard way. Both basins now price the same physical shortage, and there is no instrument left to flatten it.

The programme runs out of room before the conflict resolves
strengthened

The corridor never opened. It was never announced by Oman, its mine-clearance premise is disputed by the JMIC advisory and by Tehran, and it has been overtaken by direct US–Iran fire. With ~4.7 mb of contracted barrels and 19–28 weeks of cushion, the reserve is now measured in weeks against a conflict that has resumed rather than resolved.

→ Implication · the programme has outlasted both the deadline and the corridor. What remains is a thin buffer facing an active shooting conflict.

Three things in the DOE ledger. It reconciles — net monthly movements imply an end-2025 stock of 413.4 mb against the 413.46 mb the Bloomberg series prints. The two streams share an originator, so this is an internal-consistency check, but the cumulative-drawn figure now rests on a flow ledger rather than a difference of stocks. The return leg is empty — zero exchange-return barrels in every month from March onward, on a loan with a repayment premium, and the GAO's finding that the reserve accepts returning crude at only 56% of specification applies to a repayment leg that is entirely prospective. And one number is unresolved — DOE's legend defines a Release No. 3 that no month carries. If it sits outside the ~133.5 mb contracted, the ~4.7 mb residual understates what DOE could deliver.

What this implies

The price has detached from DOE entirely. The August path — −8% on a called-off strike, +11.5% on a conditioned reopening, then four straight sessions higher into the memorandum’s expiry — owes nothing to a reserve that drew 5.3 mb over the same stretch. Headline exposure should be sized as though the buffer were absent, because as a price-setting instrument it is. Barrels are moving; Iranian barrels are not. Aramco resumed VLCC loadings inside the strait and Murban holds a ~$7 premium to Brent, while China’s imports of Iranian crude nearly halved to 534 kb/d. The structural response is now funded: TotalEnergies backed an expansion of the Habshan–Fujairah bypass to 1.8 mb/d, roughly doubling bypass capacity within a year.

03Outlook — whose clock binds

Two clocks have stopped, one expired on 17 August, and the only one still running is physical.

The US buffer clock is now a backstop, not a countdown. ~4.7 mb of contracted deliveries remain of the ~133.5 mb awarded, with no replacement since the June tender for 40 mb drew bids on 0.5. The ~200 mb operational floor sits 86.6 mb below current stock — 19–28 weeks depending on the pace window. Commercial crude drew 4.4 mb to 424.5 mb in the week to 28 August — drawing faster than the strategic reserve, a reversal of the pattern that held through August.6 mb, with commercial crude flat at 428.9 mb in the week to 21 August — 1% above its five-year average even as the strategic reserve makes a new low. August has shown this clock no longer sets the price: crude rose 11.5% when the Oman route arrived conditioned, ran to $92.67 into the memorandum's expiry, then gave back 6.2% in three sessions on a sanctions package that spared oil and a corridor Tehran says changes nothing — none of it on DOE, which drew 9 mb over the same fortnight without moving the tape.

Which floor? Three numbers circulate. The statutory EPCA reference of 252.4 mb binds only the Secretary's limited-drawdown authority, not the exchange authority used here — now ~37 mb above current stock. DOE puts the cavern-mechanics floor at ~70 mb. This paper uses the ~200 mb Tecity operational estimate, on the view that a reserve able only to trickle barrels out of half-empty caverns is not a usable buffer — a view the GAO supports, with drawdown capability at 61% of design before ~126 mb came out. The dispute is now public: Hochstein said on 15 August that going below 300 damages the caverns; DOE rebutted that “the caverns are always full — all that changes is the ratio of oil and water.” The reserve is already below 290.

The global buffer is worse than the American one. The IEA's August report puts observed global inventories down 69 mb in July to below 7.9 bn barrels and 410 mb since the war began, ~2.7 mb/day. The US SPR's 122 mb is roughly 30% of that — one national reserve accounting for nearly a third of world destocking, which is this paper's thesis restated in someone else's data. The agency sees a 1.8 mb/day Q3 deficit, closed by cutting demand rather than finding barrels.

The diplomatic clock stopped again. The 60-day memorandum lapsed on 17 August; the corridor announced on 25–26 August was never announced by Oman and never opened. Trump declared the mines cleared on 25 August, a JMIC advisory the same day held Hormuz at SEVERE, Gharibabadi called the clearance claim “propaganda intended to create calm in global energy markets,” and CENTCOM formalised it on 28 August. Then the shooting resumed: US forces struck two IRGC rocket launchers on Larak Island on 30 August as the Guards prepared to fire rocket-delivered sea mines; on 1 September CENTCOM hit an estimated 100 IRGC targets and Iran fired 13 ballistic missiles into Jordanian airspace with 10 intercepted, plus drones on Gulf bases — the heaviest exchange in over a month. The toll booth the paper described a week ago was never built. What replaced it is ordnance.

The Iranian clock — rerouting, but not replacing. Kharg Island reactivated on 12 August after a 25-day halt and Caspian shipments from Russia have roughly tripled since the blockade began, but the volume has not followed: China’s imports of Iranian crude fell to 534 kb/d in August from 823 in July against a 1.4 mb/d 2025 average. The rial hit a record 2.02 m to the dollar on 24 August. Iran is rerouting around the blockade; the reroute is not replacing the barrels — which is the context for blacklisting 45–46 Gulf tankers and threatening confiscation.

What this implies

What remains live is the physical reopening clock — mines, escorts, insurers, ~600 stranded ships. That is the variable to watch, and transit counts read it more honestly than headlines do.

04Risk — what would confirm or falsify

The falsification test set in v2.10 fired on both legs inside one week. This is what replaces it.

The suppression thesis has substantially played out. The draw ran to a 43-year low and through it, the interim deal collapsed, and with ~4.7 mb of contracted barrels left the reserve is effectively spent. The central prediction is realised in the order specified: the barrels ran out, the mask came off at the closed strait first and the Atlantic second, and tightness surfaced in structure as the buffer emptied rather than after. The open question is no longer whether the suppression ends, but whether the market is under-pricing a strait that stays physically shut while the long end prices comfort.

Soft branch recovering

  • A signed text with maritime control resolved and mines clearing on schedule
  • Reversed the narrowing basis of the week to 26 Aug did not continue — both bases inverted while the outright rose

Hard branch winning

  • Present memorandum expired, corridor never opened, and direct US–Iran fire resumed 30 Aug–1 Sep
  • Present both basins at their widest prompt premiums on record — Brent −$3.54, Dubai −$10.08, Oman −$8.66
  • Present outright rising with the structure — $86.94 to $92.09, +5.9%
  • Present SPR still drawing — −3.1 mb to 286.6, with ~4.7 mb contracted left

Exhaustion risk — still primary, and the test needs restating. Earlier versions treated compressing backwardation as the soft branch returning. That was wrong as written: compression has arrived with a lapsed memorandum, attacked ships and vessels turning back. The signal is not the spread narrowing but which leg narrows it. Compression from a falling front is the soft branch; compression from a rising back, as now, is the market extending the disruption in time. Restated last week: only a basis narrowing while the outright falls, alongside transits sustained in the dozens, returns the soft branch. It failed decisively this week. Both bases inverted to record prompt premiums while the outright rose 5.9% — the opposite of the soft-branch signature on both legs at once. The narrowing of the week to 26 August was a pause, not a turn.

Positioning risk — beginning to correct. A curve turned at the front but anchored at the back is the classic pre-dislocation shape. The back has now begun to move: both bases compressed because the front outran spot, not because the prompt eased. That is the market pricing a failure it had treated as temporary. The correction is early — the long end still sits well below the prompt — but a market repricing the back while the strait stays shut is repricing the disruption’s duration, not its severity.

Optical-deal risk — lapsed rather than realised. The corridor was the optical branch, and it never opened: no Omani announcement, a disputed mine-clearance premise, and direct fire on top. The detection signal held throughout — the Dubai basis never normalised, and it has now gone to −$10.08.

Escalation risk. A tanker was disabled by a projectile off Oman on 24 August; Rezaei threatened to halt flows entirely if neighbouring states join the US economic campaign; and Gharibabadi warned that US mine-detection vessels would make “very good targets.” Discount definitive “strait open” claims from either side until transit counts confirm them.

What this implies

The asymmetry runs the other way from March. Then the reserve capped the upside and the risk was paying for a premium DOE could suppress. Now the front is bid, the long end is not, and there is no instrument left to flatten a dislocation. Whatever the directional view, the distribution has fattened on one side only.

A reserve sized by stock–flow thinking is now defended by exploiting the gap between stock and flow — but the same barrel cannot be both spent into the spot market and held as a buffer. The signal and the supply are spent in the same act. Conservation of matter sets the clock.

Conventions. Prices, spreads, SPR levels and gasoline come only from the Tecity Bloomberg pull; news and context are web-sourced and separately verified. Brent front-month = CO1; basis = front minus spot, so positive is contango and negative backwardation. Dubai = DBL1−DBLA. SPR = DOESSPR. Operational floor = the ~200 mb Tecity estimate (analyst Zilin), above the ~150 mb conventionally cited; cushion = level minus 200.
