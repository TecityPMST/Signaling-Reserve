What changed — week to 18 November
  • The near-stop ended, and it ended hard. DOESSPR resumed with two prints: 279.54 mb for the week to 6 November, a draw of 2.649 mb, and 276.36 mb for the week to the 13th, a draw of 3.174 mb. That is 5.8 mb in a fortnight, against 3.2 mb across the eight weeks before it combined, 0.40 mb a week. The awarded 40 mb has begun to move, in the November–December window the 29 September solicitation set.
  • The floor is a live constraint again. The cushion falls from 82.2 to 76.4 mb, and weeks-to-floor from 206–309 to 24–37. Claim 3 was restated in v3.10 around the return leg on the grounds that the floor was unreachable in any scenario. On two weeks of data that is no longer true, and the claim is restated again.
  • “We don’t need a deal with Iran.” Three days after calling the war militarily “effectively over”, the President posted that on 12 November — “We’ve got their money, Hundreds of Billions of Dollars, and we’ve got their Strait!” — and took his Vice-President off the Iran file. The post predates v3.11’s pull and this paper missed it. Al Jazeera now asks whether the war has become a frozen conflict; an ADNOC tanker was struck in the strait on the 17th, one crew member missing.
  • v3.11’s weekly point was revised, for the third time in four editions. 12 November moves from CO1 89.61 / COA 91.62 to 93.53 / 95.82 — a $3.92 revision. That row carried the intraday signature this paper has now seen three times: a high equal to the open. §04 turns it into a gate.

01Three claims, tracked weekly

Each claim carries its own evidence, its own status, and what it would imply if it holds.

The thesis, in one paragraph

In March 2026 the United States authorised a 172 mb SPR release, structured as an exchange rather than a sale, inside a coordinated 400 mb IEA action against the Hormuz closure. This paper has argued since v1 that it was never sized to replace lost supply — it cannot clear a 9–13 mb/day shortfall at any deliverable rate — and that its observable function is active price suppression: pressing the prompt market with finite barrels and the forward curve with announcement effects, to buy time for diplomacy. For eight weeks the barrels stopped while the announcements continued. This fortnight the barrels arrived, after the announcements had already been spent, into a market that no longer needed them.

The release was sized to suppress price, not to replace supply
holding

The sequencing is the evidence. The reserve delivered 5.8 mb in a fortnight — its fastest since July — and the price fell 4.7% in the same week, to the lowest since 31 August. Nothing about that is causal in either direction: the deliveries follow an award made on 29 October, into a delivery window fixed on 29 September, while the price is tracking a war the President has declared over and then declined to settle. Realised lift across the programme is 0.57 mb/day over 245 days against an advertised 1.43, still 40% of the announced rate.

→ Implication · the barrels and the price moved together this week for the first time in months, and the paper cannot claim the first caused the second. What it can say is that the instrument delivers on administrative schedules, not market ones — which is precisely why it suppresses announcements better than prices.

Suppression is visible in the prompt–physical spread
retired

Retired at v3.11 and not revived. Both legs sit in mild backwardation — Brent −2.78, Dubai −2.02 on 18 November — and the gap between them is $0.76, with four narrower sessions already this month. The spread is now describing a market with no acute prompt tightness in either grade, which is consistent with the war ending, with the deliveries arriving, and with neither. It cannot separate them, which is why it carries no claim.

→ Implication · Fig. 2 remains as description. The thesis rests on the stock series and the award record, and this week both finally have something in them.

The programme runs out of room before the conflict resolves
revived

Revived, having been retired as unreachable in v3.10. Two weeks of real deliveries cut the cushion to 76.4 mb and weeks-to-floor from 206–309 to 24–37. If the remainder of the awarded 40 mb is delivered on anything like this fortnight’s pace, the reserve reaches 236.4 mb — 16.0 mb below the 252.4 mb EPCA statutory reference and 36.4 above the house floor — inside this quarter. The return leg remains at zero since the first quarter against 216 mb promised from early next year.

→ Implication · the question this paper retired six weeks ago is live again on two data points. It should be held at revived, not holding, until a third week shows the pace is a trend rather than a batch.

What this implies

For eight weeks this paper reported a reserve that had stopped moving while its programme was declared complete. The barrels were not cancelled; they were queued. What arrived in a fortnight is roughly an eighth of the 40 mb awarded, which puts the rest inside the next two months — and takes the reserve to a level the EPCA reference has something to say about.

03Outlook — whose clock binds

The buffer clock has restarted after eight weeks stopped, and it is the only one that moved this week.

The buffer clock has restarted. DOESSPR resumed with two prints after the Veterans Day deferral: 279.535 mb for the week to 6 November and 276.361 mb for the week to the 13th. The cushion above the ~200 mb house floor is 76.4 mb, against 82.2 a fortnight ago. At the last week’s draw the floor is 24 weeks away; at the three-week average, 37. Both numbers were above two hundred in the last edition. The statutory EPCA reference of 252.4 mb now sits only 24.0 mb below stock, and delivering the balance of the awarded 40 mb would take the reserve to 236.4 mb — 16.0 mb below that reference. The 279.5 figure this paper declined to use last week, on the grounds that it post-dated the pull, is confirmed by the workbook at 279.535.

The policy clock is still silent. No DOE publication in the window: no awardee names, no delivery confirmations, no return dates, no new solicitation. The deliveries visible in the stock series are therefore inferred from inventory alone, which is the proxy this paper has flagged all year. What is new is that the proxy finally has a signal in it — and that it arrived inside the November–December window the 29 September RFP published, which remains the only schedule DOE has ever put in writing.

The war clock went backwards a week ago, not this week. On 12 November — three days after calling the war militarily “effectively over”, and the same day as v3.11’s pull — the President posted on Truth Social: “We don’t need a deal with Iran. Those days have long since passed. We’ve got their money, Hundreds of Billions of Dollars, and we’ve got their Strait!” The same post directed Vice-President Vance to give his “complete and total attention” to domestic corruption, taking him off the Iran file. Parliament speaker Ghalibaf, Iran’s chief negotiator, had said on the 11th that “the ball is now in the American court” and that the strait would “never return” to toll-free. Both predate v3.11 and belong in its record, not this one. What is genuinely this week: Araghchi told the ECO meeting in Islamabad on the 16th that the bloc backs reopening the strait in two days rather than a week, conditional on sanctions relief, release of frozen funds and an end to the US naval blockade; Al Jazeera asked on the 17th whether this is now a frozen conflict; and on the same day an ADNOC L&S vessel was struck in the strait, one crew member missing and eleven injured. Attacks on shipping there run at more than one a day. Kpler counted five commodity vessels through Hormuz on 7 November and none on the 8th.

And the return clock has not started. 216 mb is due “beginning early next year”, including 44 mb of premium DOE valued at more than $4 billion. Nothing has returned since the first quarter. The flat price is now $89.17, the lowest since 31 August, which makes those barrels cheap for whoever owes them — and the premium, booked above $100, worth less than DOE published.

Physical

For the first time since September the reserve is doing something measurable. Whether it continues is the one question this paper can answer from its own data, and the answer arrives weekly.

Products. On the Bloomberg series US average retail gasoline printed $4.67 on 17 November, down from the $5.35 peak of 24 September and falling in every session of the week. It has now given back the entire war premium and more, which is the political variable the midterms turned on and the one the administration has least reason to disturb.

What this implies

Three questions carry the next edition: whether a third week confirms the delivery pace, whether the reserve crosses the EPCA reference before the return leg starts, and whether a frozen conflict stays frozen while the instrument that was supposed to thaw it is being spent down.

04Risk — what would confirm or falsify

A third revision to a same-day row turns an observation into a gate. That is recorded first.

v3.11’s weekly point was revised by $3.92. 12 November moves from CO1 89.61 / COA 91.62 to 93.53 / 95.82. It is the third same-day final row to be revised in four editions, after 29 October and 5 November, and all three shared a signature visible at the time: the session high equalled the open. A row showing no trade above its opening price is a row captured before the session finished. In the 12 November pull that row read O 90.25, H 90.25, L 89.51, C 89.61, and its range was a fifth of the trailing twenty-session median. It is now O 90.25, H 93.96, L 89.22, C 93.53.

Gate 9, adopted this edition. On every pull, test the final row: if the high equals the open, or the high–low range is below ~0.35× the trailing twenty-session median, treat that row as provisional and move the weekly point back to the most recent session that satisfies Gate 9 and hazard 13. This edition applies it: 19 November scores narrow (range $0.90, 0.27× the median) and has no reported settle, so the weekly point is 18 November — which reconciles exactly with an independently reported Brent settle of $91.95, a fall of $1.19 and 1.28%, matching the pull on level, dollar change and percentage. The 19 November figures (CO1 89.32, COA 92.14) are recorded here and not plotted.

Delivery risk — two points are not a trend. A fortnight at 0.42 mb/day could be a batch clearing rather than a run rate: the 29 October award covered more than 40 mb and roughly an eighth of it has appeared. If the pace holds, the balance lands inside two months and the reserve passes the EPCA reference in January; if it was a batch, the series returns to the near-stop and claim 3 is retired again. A third consecutive week above 2 mb is the test, and it arrives with the next pull.

Return risk — unchanged and compounding. Nothing has come back since the first quarter. Every barrel delivered now is a barrel that must be repurchased later, and the price at which it would be repurchased has fallen 13.5% below the level at which the programme was announced. That is cheaper for the counterparty and worse for the premium DOE has booked, and DOE has still published no valuation date, no counterparty and no return schedule.

Measurement risk. The 18 November session is the cleanest column evidence yet. Reuters reports Brent settling $91.95, −$1.19, −1.28% and WTI $83.26, −$2.75, −3.2%, lowest since 26 August; COA and CLA reproduce all seven of those figures exactly, CO1 and CL1 none. That takes COA to ten matched prints and CLA to four. On the revised data the two columns still disagree about the programme’s outcome — CO1 13.5% below its 13 March level, COA 1.0% above — which remains the strongest argument for restating the archive onto COA, and the reason this paper publishes both.

What this implies

The week reverses the direction of the paper’s central question. For two months the finding was that a reserve declared complete had stopped moving. The finding now is that it is moving faster than at any time since July, towards a floor it was recently said to be unable to reach — and still with nothing coming back.

A reserve sized by stock–flow thinking was defended by exploiting the gap between stock and flow. For eight weeks the gap was all stock and no flow, and this paper said so. This fortnight the flow arrived — on an administrative schedule, after the announcements were spent, into a market that had already repriced without it. The barrels are leaving on a timetable set in September, and the only number that has never moved is the one measuring what comes back.
