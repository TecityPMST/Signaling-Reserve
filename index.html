What changed — week to 25 November
  • The test was met. DOESSPR printed 273.465 mb for the week to 20 November, a draw of 2.896 mb and a third straight week above two. The three weeks total 8.719 mb against 0.397 a week across the eight weeks of the near-stop. Weeks-to-floor now reads 25 on the last draw and 25 on the three-week average — the two measures have converged, which is what a steady pace looks like.
  • Gate 9 worked on its first outing. v3.12 marked 19 November provisional on the range test and declined to plot it. The new pull revised it — CO1 89.32→89.44, COA 92.14→91.98. The gate caught a bad row before it was published, which is what the three prior revisions cost the paper to learn.
  • And hazard 9 caught v3.12 anyway. The WTI duplicate run has extended over 18–20 November, overwriting CLA with CL1’s closes. The 83.26 that v3.12 cited as an exact match to a reported WTI settle no longer exists in the column; that evidence is withdrawn in §04. The case itself got stronger: 23, 24 and 25 November deliver six fresh exact matches.
  • Diplomacy restarted, through Qatar, while the shooting intensified. A Qatari delegation spent two hours in Tehran on 20 November; Iran says it is in contact with mediators but not negotiating, and Araghchi now offers expert-level talks and a strait reopened in “two hours” if the seven conditions are met. In the same week UKMTO logged attacks on Gulf shipping, three ships were struck on the 23rd, and the Houthis hit two Saudi refineries.

01Three claims, tracked weekly

Each claim carries its own evidence, its own status, and what it would imply if it holds.

The thesis, in one paragraph

In March 2026 the United States authorised a 172 mb SPR release, structured as an exchange rather than a sale, inside a coordinated 400 mb IEA action against the Hormuz closure. This paper has argued since v1 that it was never sized to replace lost supply — it cannot clear a 9–13 mb/day shortfall at any deliverable rate — and that its observable function is active price suppression: pressing the prompt market with finite barrels and the forward curve with announcement effects, to buy time for diplomacy. The barrels are now moving at the fastest rate since August. They are moving into a market that priced Libya, a surplus forecast and two refinery attacks, and barely registered them.

The release was sized to suppress price, not to replace supply
holding

The week is a controlled experiment and the reserve loses it. Three weeks of the programme’s fastest deliveries since August coincided with a flat price that fell 3.46% on Libya’s Sharara restart and a surplus call, then rose 3.51% on a Houthi claim against Ras Tanura, and finished the week up 1.6%. Every move the market made was attributable to something other than the reserve. The one voice placing the reserve in the price is UBS, which told clients that releases from strategic reserves “had helped offset lower Middle Eastern exports” — an offset, not a floor. Realised lift is 0.56 mb/day over 252 days, 39% of the advertised 1.43.

→ Implication · the instrument is doing what this paper said it does: supplying a marginal offset that competes with Libyan restarts and surplus forecasts, while the announcements that once moved the curve are spent. Suppression at the margin is not the same as suppression, and the distinction is now visible weekly.

Suppression is visible in the prompt–physical spread
retired

Retired at v3.11 and still retired. The Brent basis widened to −3.97 on 25 November, the deepest backwardation since 30 October, and Dubai to −2.95. Read nothing into it yet: the January Brent contract — the one the wires quote and the one the COA column carries — expires at the end of this month, so part of that widening is the roll and the pull cannot separate it. Hazard 11 applies.

→ Implication · Fig. 2 stays descriptive. The next pull spans the roll, and a basis reading taken across it will be reported with that caveat attached rather than as a signal.

The programme runs out of room before the conflict resolves
holding

Promoted from revived. The third week settles the question v3.12 left open: at 2.896 mb this is a pace, not a batch, and the two estimates of time-to-floor have converged on 25 weeks. The cushion is 73.5 mb. The 252.4 mb EPCA statutory reference now sits only 21.1 mb below stock, and delivering the balance of the awarded 40 mb would take the reserve to 233.5 mb, 18.9 mb below it. The return leg is still at zero since the first quarter against 216 mb promised from early next year.

→ Implication · on this pace the reserve crosses the statutory reference around the turn of the year, before a single barrel has come back. That is the collision this claim has been pointing at since v1, and it is now inside the forecast horizon rather than beyond it.

What this implies

Three weeks at this pace put roughly a fifth of the awarded 40 mb into the market and took the reserve to a level it has reportedly not seen since the Reagan administration was still filling it. The remaining question is no longer whether the barrels are moving. It is whether anything stops them before the statutory reference.

03Outlook — whose clock binds

The buffer clock is running fast and steady; the policy clock has gone quiet for four weeks.

The buffer clock is steady, and that is the news. 273.465 mb for the week to 20 November, a draw of 2.896 mb after 3.174 and 2.649. The cushion above the ~200 mb house floor is 73.5 mb; at the last draw the floor is 25 weeks away and at the three-week average 25, the first time the two measures have agreed since the week to 22 May. The reserve is at its lowest since 1982. The year is well established — wire reports have carried that framing since at least September, when the level was 285.4 mb — but the month is contested: World Oil Monitor puts the match at August 1982 (272.6 mb), while those wire reports, written at higher levels, said November 1982. The paper uses the year and leaves the month open.

The policy clock has been silent for four weeks. Nothing from DOE since the 29 October award: no awardee names, no delivery confirmations, no return dates, no new solicitation. The silence is a choice, not a capacity problem — DOE published a Northeast Home Heating Oil Reserve award on 4 November, so the press office is working. What it has never published is a delivery confirmation under the November–December window its own 29 September solicitation set. The only delivery figure DOE has ever given is 35 mb, stated on 11 May, in aggregate, six months stale.

The war clock moved in both directions at once. The mediation channel reopened at both ends and delivered nothing. On 19 November, in Berlin, Witkoff and Kushner met Qatar’s prime minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani and his envoy Ali al-Thawadi; Axios reported no major progress. A Qatari delegation then held two hours of talks in Tehran on the night of 20 November; deputy foreign minister Gharibabadi said on the 21st that Iran is “in contact with mediators, including the Qataris, both in person and virtually” but that these are not yet negotiations. Araghchi, the same day, offered expert-level talks on implementation and said the two-day reopening plan is flexible — “from two days to one day or even two hours, that is not an issue for us” — while insisting the seven conditions come first and declaring the Islamabad memorandum “no longer on the table”. His public line stayed hard: “as long as Iran’s demands are not met, the discussion on the Strait of Hormuz and its opening is pointless.” Trump, on the 21st: “We make a deal, and then they break the deal the next day.” Meanwhile UKMTO logged attacks on Gulf shipping on 21–22 November, Bahrain said an Iranian strike hit a civilian industrial facility, the IRGC said it fired missiles and drones at US positions there, three ships were struck transiting the strait on the 23rd, a container ship struck a mine on the 24th, and the Houthis claimed the Rabigh refinery on the 22nd and Ras Tanura on the 25th.

Soft
reopened
A mediated channel exists again and Iran has named a smaller ask — talks about implementation rather than terms. It is still indirect, still conditional on seven unmet demands, and Tehran says plainly it is not negotiating.
Optical

Unchanged. The release is complete, the war has been declared over once, and the price now moves on Libyan pipelines and Houthi drones. Nothing is left to announce that the market has not already heard.
Physical
binding
Three weeks of real deliveries, a four-decade low in the stock, and 25 weeks of cushion. This is the branch with a date on it.

Products. US average retail gasoline printed $4.50 on 24 November, the lowest since 14 July and down from the $5.35 peak of 24 September. It has fallen in every session since 13 November. The war premium at the pump is gone, which removes the political reason to stop drawing and leaves only the statutory one.

What this implies

The two clocks have swapped places. For two months the policy clock was the live one and the stock series had nothing in it; now the stock series is the only thing moving and DOE has gone quiet. A reserve that crosses its statutory reference while the department that owns it publishes nothing is the finding this paper will be tracking into January.

04Risk — what would confirm or falsify

One gate proved itself this week and one hazard forced a withdrawal. Both are recorded first.

Gate 9 was right. v3.12 adopted it after three same-day rows were revised, applied it to 19 November — range $0.90, 0.27× the trailing-20 median, no reported settle — and moved the weekly point back to the 18th. The 26 November pull revised 19 November: CO1 89.32→89.44, COA 92.14→91.98, CL1 and CLA likewise, with Dubai, Oman and gasoline filling in from blank. Had the paper plotted that row it would be restating it now. Applied again this week, 26 November is provisional — range $0.65, 0.18× the median, the third-narrowest ratio of the year — so the weekly point is 25 November, which reconciles exactly with a reported settle.

Hazard 9 recurred, and v3.12’s WTI evidence is withdrawn. The duplicate run in which CL1’s close overwrites CLA has extended from 33 sessions to 36, and the new sessions are 18, 19 and 20 November. v3.12 reported that CLA’s 83.26 on 18 November reproduced a Reuters WTI settle exactly and counted it as a fourth matched print. That cell now reads 82.31, which is CL1’s close. The match was real when it was published and the column has since been overwritten, exactly as happened to v3.7’s 16 October evidence. The print is withdrawn and the count reduced. This is the second time the same artefact has retracted the same kind of claim, and the rule it generates is in §05.

Delivery risk — the pace is established, the endpoint is not. Roughly a fifth of the awarded 40 mb has moved. If the rest follows at 2.9 mb a week it is gone by mid-January and the reserve passes the statutory reference on the way. The falsifier is now the opposite of last week’s: a week back under 1 mb would mean the award is being delivered in tranches rather than continuously, and claim 3 would go back to contested. Watch the week to 27 November, which spans the Thanksgiving holiday and may print short for calendar reasons alone.

Return risk — unchanged and compounding. Nothing has come back since the first quarter. Every barrel delivered now is one that must be repurchased later, and the repurchase price sits 12% below the level at which the programme was announced. DOE has published no valuation date, no counterparty and no return schedule, and the 44 mb premium it valued above $4 billion is worth materially less at $90 than at the prices that produced the figure.

Measurement risk. Three consecutive sessions produced six exact wire matches: Brent settles of $89.37 (−$3.20, −3.46%), $91.40 (+$2.03, +2.27%) and $94.61 (+$3.21, +3.5%) on 23–25 November all reproduce from COA on level, dollar change and percentage, and the matching WTI settles of $80.17, $82.05 and $83.68 all reproduce from CLA. CO1 and CL1 match none of the six. That takes COA to thirteen matched prints and CLA, after the withdrawal above, to six. The wires also name the contract — “January Brent” — which is the clearest confirmation yet that COA is the front month and that front minus spot is a calendar spread. The columns still disagree about the programme’s outcome: CO1 12.1% below March, COA 3.9% above.

What this implies

The paper’s two standing measurement problems moved in opposite directions this week. The column identification is now as near certain as external evidence can make it, and the column that carries it is the one the archive does not use. The case for restating onto COA is stronger than the case for leaving it, and the only argument left for leaving it is that published PDFs cannot be rewritten.

The question this paper asked in March was whether a reserve could be spent on a signal. Three weeks of the fastest deliveries since August have now passed into a market that moved on a Libyan pipeline and two refinery drones, and barely noticed. The signal was spent long ago. What is being spent now is the reserve, on a timetable written in September, towards a statutory line that arrives before the first barrel comes back.
